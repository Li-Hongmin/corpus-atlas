# Scientific investigations

Prefer recent primary studies, authoritative reviews, and essential foundational work. Record study
design, population or system, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcome, uncertainty, and known
failure boundary. Use citation graphs for discovery, not as evidence. Distinguish metadata-only,
abstract-screened, and full-text-read sources. Check corrections and retractions when consequential.
Use the retrieval ladder in order: reuse the local record; query available scholarly connectors;
discover with OpenAlex or Crossref; resolve important records against DOI, PubMed, arXiv, publisher,
or repository pages; then retrieve explicitly licensed open files. Cache metadata, abstracts,
references, and citations even if retrieval fails. Record main text and supplements independently.

Build maps concentrically from anchor papers through backward references, forward citations, recent
search windows, neighboring terminology, and searches designed to challenge the emerging account.
Stop a branch at conceptual saturation, not an arbitrary paper count.
